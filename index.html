01 · The premise

Models produce outputs. Agents produce consequences.

Traditional model risk focuses on data, methodology, and predictive performance. Agentic AI can interpret instructions, retrieve information, call tools, and change financial or operational state. Its control environment must extend beyond the model.

01

Act within authority

Know who delegated authority, which actions are permitted, which limits apply, and when human approval is required.

02

Separate evidence from inference

03

Leave a verifiable trace

Preserve what was proposed, which controls ran, what was approved, and what ultimately occurred.

03 · Methods

Separate where risk originates from how it appears.

Misuse, misalignment, and mistake are sources of agent risk. Failure modes are observable manifestations. One failure mode may arise from several sources.

R1

Misuse

The agent is directed, manipulated, or repurposed to act improperly or outside legitimate authority.

R2

Misalignment

The agent pursues an objective, proxy, or interpretation that diverges from the principal’s authorized intent.

R3

Mistake

The agent pursues an authorized objective incorrectly because of faulty information, reasoning, calculation, or execution.

Selected failure modes

An evolving—not exhaustive—taxonomy.

F1Context rot

Long-running work accumulates stale or distracting context until the agent loses the governing objective. Controls include context budgets, deliberate compression, durable state, and periodic restatement of mandate.

F2Doom loops

The agent repeats a failing action without learning or escalating. Controls include retry limits, loop detection, alternate-path requirements, and human interruption.

F3Tool sprawl

Overlapping tools and ambiguous descriptions increase inappropriate capability selection. Controls include minimal tool sets, clear contracts, permission boundaries, and tool-use tests.

F4Prompt injection

Untrusted content is interpreted as instruction. Controls require provenance, content isolation, least privilege, policy enforcement, and approval for consequential actions.

F5Overconfident completion

The agent reports success without evidence that the intended outcome occurred. Controls include independent verification, execution receipts, state reconciliation, and explicit uncertainty.

F6Compounding cost

Recursive delegation or unnecessary model calls consume resources without proportional value. Controls include budgets, routing, caching, stopping rules, and cost-aware evaluation.

F7Silent capability drift

Changes to models, tools, data, or prompts alter behavior without a visible business-process change. Controls include version binding, regression suites, change approval, and monitoring.

Risk origin→Failure mode→Control→Evidence

A fiduciary control is more than a written expectation. The agent should leave evidence that the relevant control operated when a consequential decision was made.

The operating thesis

Trustworthy AI will depend not only on better models, but on better environments around them.
